# About Scrooge

Every London council has to publish each payment it makes over £500. Some publish
everything over £250. The rule has been in place for more than a decade, and the
data is public. In practice almost nobody reads it, because it sits in thousands
of spreadsheets spread over 33 council websites, and no two councils lay their
spreadsheet out the same way.

Scrooge gathers those spreadsheets into one place and lets you ask about them in
plain English. We built it at the
[Tech Europe agentic AI hack](https://hackathons.techeurope.io/events/tech-europe-agentic-ai-hack)
on 19 September 2026.

## How it works

There are three steps, and only the last one involves AI.

**Collecting.** A program visits each council's transparency page and downloads
every spending file it finds, exactly as the council published it. We keep the
originals so any figure can be traced back to the file it came from.

**Tidying.** One council calls a column "Supplier", another "Vendor Name", a
third "Payee". Dates and amounts come in a dozen formats. A second program reads
each layout and writes every payment into one shared table with the same columns:
who paid, who got paid, how much, when, and what the council said it was for.

**Answering.** When you type a question, an AI model works out which lookups it
needs, such as "total for Camden in 2024" or "largest payments to this supplier".
It can only choose from seven fixed lookups. It cannot change the data, and it
does not write its own database queries. The results come back as the tables and
charts you see in the chat, and the model writes the sentences around them.

That split matters when you check an answer. The numbers in the cards come
straight from the council's files. The sentences come from the AI. If the two
disagree, believe the card.

## What you can ask today

Scrooge holds **15 of London's 33 boroughs**, about **15 million payments** that
add up to roughly £83 billion:

- Barnet, Bexley, Brent, Camden, Haringey
- Havering, Hounslow, Islington, Lambeth, Lewisham
- Newham, Redbridge, Richmond, Wandsworth, Westminster

How far back it goes depends on the council. Barnet, Hounslow and Lambeth have
more than ten years of payments. Haringey and Lewisham start in 2023. The newest
payments are from summer 2026.

Questions that work well:

- How much did a borough spend in a given year, and on what?
- Who are a borough's biggest suppliers?
- How much has one company been paid, by one council or by all of them?
- What were the largest single payments?
- How do two boroughs compare?

If you are not sure what is covered, ask "what data do you have?" and Scrooge
will list each borough with its date range.

## Limitations

**More than half of London is missing.** Eighteen boroughs are not in yet. Five
of them put their files behind bot protection, and we have chosen not to work
around that. The other thirteen are researched and waiting for someone to write
their reader.

**It is not the whole of a council's spending.** Payments under the £500 or £250
threshold are not published. Neither are salaries, and councils redact payments
to individuals, such as foster carers or people receiving care budgets. A
borough's total here will be lower than the figure in its annual accounts.

**Supplier names are as the council typed them.** The same company can appear as
"Veolia", "Veolia ES (UK) Ltd" and "VEOLIA ENVIRONMENTAL". Scrooge matches on the
text, so it can miss a spelling, and it can match too much. Ask about Capita and
it also picks up every payment described as "capital".

**Categories do not line up between councils.** Each council has its own
departments and its own labels for what a payment was for. Comparing total spend
between boroughs is sound. Comparing "housing" in one with "housing" in another
is rough.

**Lists stop at 50 rows.** Totals and rankings are worked out over every payment,
but a list of individual payments shows 50 at most.

**The source files have mistakes in them.** A handful of Redbridge payments are
dated in the 2030s. Some councils publish refunds as negative amounts, which we
keep as they are. We fix how we read a file, never what the council wrote.

**The AI can misread a question.** It may pick the wrong year or the wrong
borough, or give up on a question that needs too many lookups. Check that the
card shows what you meant to ask. Scrooge also forgets everything when you start
a new chat.

## What is next: budgets

Knowing what a council spent is half the story. The other half is what it said
it would spend.

We already download the budget side: the returns every council files with central
government each year, which cover all 33 boroughs, the London council tax
figures, and the full budget books of ten boroughs. None of it is in the database
yet, so Scrooge cannot answer budget questions today.

The plan is to load those figures next to the payments, so you can ask whether a
borough spent more on temporary accommodation than it budgeted, or how its
planned spending on adult social care has moved over five years. Budget books
are long PDFs rather than spreadsheets, so that work is harder than the payments
were.

After budgets come the thirteen boroughs still waiting for a reader.

## Source and contact

Scrooge is open source under the MIT licence. The code, the research on where
each borough publishes, and the list of known problems are on
[GitHub](https://github.com/Ambit-Labs/scrooge). If a figure looks wrong, an
issue there is the fastest way to tell us.
